## cat=The big ones

?? How was basic?

Honestly? Reception nearly broke me and the back half I loved. Those are both true and they're about six weeks apart.

The first three days were the worst days of my life to that point. 36+ hours awake, 20 degree nights, blood draws, a kid trying to kill himself in front of me. I wrote that I'd go back in time and tell myself not to go. By March I was writing that it felt like home and I was almost sad it was over.

The thing nobody tells you is that it isn't mostly hard. It's mostly boring, and cold, and hungry, with about five minutes of actual soldiering scattered through a sixteen hour day. The hard parts I could take. It was the dullness and the shivering that got me.

## cat=The cold

?? How cold was it, really?

South Carolina had its coldest winter in a long time and we were in it in short sleeves. 21 degrees the day I landed. 16 degree mile runs that felt like 5 with wind. 18 degrees the Sunday they cancelled church because it was too icy. Guys' glasses froze to their faces.

By the end I had nerve damage in my hands and toes and one toe went entirely white and numb — I could bang it into a dresser as hard as I could and feel nothing.

?? Did people actually get hurt from the cold?

Yes. Constantly. Three kids went down from hypothermia on one Sunday. Two more got hospitalized before the Anvil. One kid had to leave basic entirely — he shivered so hard that apparently his bones ground together and fed bone marrow into his bloodstream and liver, and he started having seizures.

They gave us a brief on cold weather illness prevention, which was funny, because they wouldn't let us wear layers.

?? Why couldn't you just wear more layers?

Because sniffle gear wasn't authorized and they threatened an Article 15 over it, on the theory that we'd overheat. So we had casualties instead.

I started breaking uniform code in week two and never stopped. Extra t-shirt, shorts, long PT pants underneath. Got caught once with two shirts and did ten push-ups for it.

Let trainees have the same thermals the drill sergeants are wearing. I wrote a whole promise to myself about it and I meant it.

## cat=Food and hunger

?? Were you hungry the whole time?

Yes. I lost about 20 pounds and got down to a 20.2 BMI, which is barely over the 20 you need to qualify for double rations. I got the double rations in March, five weeks after I started asking.

A representative breakfast: one slice of white bread, a piece of grilled chicken the size of a nugget, an orange, a Nutri-Grain bar, and an ashtray amount of eggs. One dinner was a chicken nugget, four leaves of shredded lettuce, and an apple.

?? What does hunger do to you mentally?

It flattens you into an animal. Between the hunger and standing in the cold for hours you can't hold a thought — you just cycle through urges.

I grew up with sleep for dinner, so hunger wasn't new to me. Starving people is still the most effective way I've seen to break someone.

Five hours on a good night, less with fire guard. Wake up was usually 0430 to 0500, sometimes 0315. During reception it was worse than that — 40 hours awake before we got 40 minutes down.

Not a bad gig, honestly. Up in the dark with a red headlamp, sweeping a floor that has nothing on it, waking the next guy in ten minutes. It was the only real privacy I had, so most of the long philosophical entries in here were written on fire guard.
